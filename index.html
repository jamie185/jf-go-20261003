Pokémon Go got people walking.
For about six weeks.

The early bump is well documented, and so is how quickly it faded. JF Go leans on the things that held up longer in step trials: walking that counts anywhere, goals that fit the person, and a team to walk for.

+955

extra steps a day in week one for players, against non-players. No longer evident by week six.

Howe et al., BMJ 2016. 1,182 adults, observational.
+1,473

extra steps a day for the most engaged players over their first month, about 26% more.

Althoff et al., JMIR 2016. 1,420 players among 31,793 wearable users.
+920

extra steps a day over 24 weeks, in the competition arm of a behaviourally designed game.

STEP UP trial, JAMA Intern Med 2019. 602 adults, randomised.
+868

extra steps a day over 12 months when a game was combined with small rewards.

BE ACTIVE trial, Circulation 2024. 1,062 adults, randomised.

The longer trials point at progress from ordinary walking, goals relative to your own normal, and a small group. Those are the bones of JF Go. The map and the camera are there to get people started, and the pilot will tell us if they do.

Four things.
All built around walking.

It lives inside the Challenge tab you already have, as the live mode of the club map. Same points, levels, inventory and JFmoji.

Drops

Three a day, on footpaths inside real parks, 400 m to 1.5 km from your home area. You have to be there to open one. No ticking it off from the couch.

  • Shown in steps, not just metres. The camera is optional.
  • A shared rare drop each week for your gym
  • An optional "10 push-ups here?" bonus

Crates

Drops give sealed crates. Crates open with steps walked anywhere: 2,000, 5,000 or 10,000. The dog walk counts. The shops count.

  • Inspired by Pokémon's eggs: progress from everyday walking
  • A free crate every Monday, so normal walking always pays
  • Inside: trinkets, JFmoji wearables, points

Blocks

Your walks paint the map in your gym's colour. A block belongs to whichever gym walked it most this week, so it fades unless people keep walking there.

  • Gym against gym, scored fairly for small gyms
  • Never shows a person, only a gym colour
  • Your home block never records you

Gym drops

Gabrielle's idea. As a booked session ends, something's hidden in the gym, worth 3×. A small JF plaque on a machine, moved weekly by the trainer. The camera finds it.

  • Shows as the session ends, so nobody's on their phone mid-set
  • The clue is a photo of the corner
  • Last week's code expires, and a scan only counts after your own session there

Can we actually do AR?
Yes. Here's how far.

The meal scanner already uses the camera inside JF Coach, and Three.js is already in the app. So the first beam runs in the web layer. It needs the location permission in a native release, and testing on real phones.

The beam

Camera, compass and GPS in the web app. A column of light points toward the drop, an arrow turns you to it, and the trinket appears when you're within 20 m. That's the page linked above. Try it on your phone outside.

Needs the location permission in a native release, then device testing.
Version 2

Pinned to the machine

Native ARKit and ARCore recognise the JF plaque, so the trinket sits on the machine and stays put as you move. On phones with depth sensing it can hide behind the machine too.

A custom Capacitor plugin. About 2 weeks for an iPhone prototype, 5 to 8 weeks for both platforms.
Version 3

The gym in 3D

A trainer films a slow walk around one area of the gym. That becomes a 3D scan (Gaussian splat) you can look around in the app. It could double as a "see the gym before your free session" tour for leads.

Half a day to a day per area once the pipeline works.

Territory,
for gyms.

The app you were thinking of is INTVL, from Melbourne: close a loop and the ground inside is yours. It claims 2.5 million downloads. Its reviews say it gets people "out and about walking", and also that it's "very bro-ey and combative", "needs to be loads simpler", and that runners wipe out walkers. So JF Go keeps the feeling and fixes those.

Blocks, not loops. The map is cut into hexagons about 350 m across. Walking through counts. You don't need to plan a loop, so any walk helps.
Held by walking, not by being first. A block belongs to the gym with the most steps in it over the last 7 days. Stop walking there and it fades back to grey.
Fair to small gyms. Weekly score = blocks held ÷ √members. Chelsea Heights' 18 walkers can beat a gym of 40.
Built for privacy. Gym colours only, a day late, never names or routes. A block only shows once three or more people from that gym have walked it. Your home block and the ring around it never record your walks.

Built around the walk.
Not a video game.

Plain words, real places, one reminder a day at most. No battles, no factions, no streak guilt.

Fair play

  • You have to be there. GPS within about 40 m, accuracy 65 m or better.
  • On foot for the last 300 m. Walking speed, no 25 km/h segments.
  • Steps have to match. At least 60% of the steps the distance needs.
  • Drove most of the way? Walk the last bit and it still counts, at half points.
  • Locations the phone marks as faked are rejected. A determined cheat can still get through, so prizes stay small.

Safety

  • Public open space only: park paths, reserves, trails. From OpenStreetMap.
  • Never on roads, rail, water edges, schools, playgrounds or private land. Every candidate spot gets a human check before launch.
  • Daylight only. Nothing shows above 20 km/h.
  • No nudges in rain, heat over 32° or at night.
  • Accessibility: set your furthest drop and step-free places only.

Privacy

  • Home area is a suburb, never an address.
  • Routes stay on the phone. A claim sends only the last few hundred metres, and block totals.
  • Claim points are deleted after 30 days.
  • Trainers see drops collected and steps, never places or routes.
  • The existing map privacy tests extend to drops and blocks.

What it takes.

A reference rules engine is written and passes its tests (below). The real build is wiring it into the live API, storage and Apple Health, the native location permission, the app screens, and a careful pilot.

The name

"JF Go" is the working name, from your message. Inside the app it's just "Go" on the club map. Drops, crates and blocks are the only new words.

JF+ or everyone?

Suggested: drops, crates and blocks free for every client, because steps matter for everyone. JF+ adds gold drops, a fourth crate slot and the seasonal sets.

Plaques in Snap gyms

Gym drops need a small sticker on a machine in each club. That probably needs a yes from each Snap owner.

Map secrets: stay virtual?

Right now the hidden challenges on the map can be ticked from home, by design. Go drops fix that for the new layer. Want the old secrets location-checked too?
